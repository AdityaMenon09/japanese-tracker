## Part 1 — The exam you are actually sitting

N5 is **90 minutes** in three papers:

| Paper | Time | Scored out of |
|---|---|---|
| 言語知識（文字・語彙） | 20 min | — |
| 言語知識（文法）・読解 | 40 min | 120 (with 文字・語彙) |
| 聴解 | 30 min | 60 |

**Pass = 80 / 180 overall**, *and* you must clear both sectional minimums:
**38 / 120** on 言語知識・読解 and **19 / 60** on 聴解. Clearing the total but failing
a section is still a fail — which is why listening cannot be left to the end.

Those three papers break into **thirteen 大問**. This is the useful unit: the exam
doesn't test "grammar", it tests three specific grammar formats.

| Paper | 大問 | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| 文字・語彙 | 漢字読み | word in kanji → its reading |
| | 表記 | word in kana → its kanji |
| | 文脈規定 | which word fits the sentence |
| | 言い換え類義 | which sentence means the same |
| 文法 | 文法形式の判断 | fill the blank |
| | 文の組み立て | order fragments, name the ★ one |
| | 文章の文法 | the blank that fits a passage's flow |
| 読解 | 内容理解（短文） | ~80字 note |
| | 内容理解（中文） | ~250字 text |
| | 情報検索 | find one fact in a ~250字 notice |
| 聴解 | 課題理解 | what does the person do next? |
| | ポイント理解 | listen for one stated point |
| | 発話表現 | what do you say here? |
| | 即時応答 | pick the reply |

Source: the official [大問のねらい table](https://www.jlpt.jp/guideline/pdf/n5.pdf) and
[test-sections page](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/testsections.html).

> **Ignore the "25 min / 50 min" timings you will find on most study blogs.** Those are
> pre-2022. The live official page says 20 / 40 / 30.

## Part 2 — What the app does, mapped to those 大問

Every one of the thirteen is now trained. **423 drillable items.**

| App section | Trains | Items |
|---|---|---:|
| **文字・語彙 drills** | 漢字読み, 表記, 文脈規定, 言い換え類義 | 130 |
| **Grammar drills** | 文法形式の判断, 文の組み立て, 文章の文法 | 242 |
| **Reading practice** | 短文, 中文, 情報検索 | 8 passages |
| **Listening** | 課題理解, ポイント理解, 発話表現, 即時応答 | 43 |
| **Mock exam** | all thirteen, in the paper's own order | 37 per run |

Supporting sections, which train the underlying knowledge rather than a format:
**Lessons** (the grammar itself), **Flashcards** (SRS vocabulary), **Quiz**,
**Kana chart**, **Vocabulary by theme**, **Sentence of the day**, **Writing practice**.

### The sections you'll use most

**Lessons** — the Minna no Nihongo I order, 1–25. Each lesson now *teaches*: the rule in
plain English, conjugation and pattern tables, and a **Watch out** box of the specific
traps. 100 teaching points, 26 tables, 60 pitfall notes. Each lesson ends with a
**"Drill lesson N →"** button that jumps straight to grammar questions for that lesson.

**Grammar drills** — 200 fill-in-the-blank items, **8 per lesson across all 25**, plus
25 sentence-assembly items and 4 cloze passages. Pick a lesson to drill what you just
read, or *All 25 lessons* for a real test. Only the all-lessons run records a best score,
so a narrow 8-question set can't flatter you.

**文字・語彙 drills** — the paper everyone forgets. Distractors are written to trap the
real confusions: 病院 misread as びよういん, 天気 as でんき, 聞 against 間 / 開 / 門.

**Listening** — needs a Japanese voice. **Your phone has one; this laptop's Chrome does
not.** Microsoft Edge also works. On a device without one the section explains itself
instead of silently disappearing.

**Mock exam** — 37 questions across all thirteen 大問 in the real order, on a 25-minute
timer, scored per paper section *and* broken down per 大問 underneath. That breakdown is
the point: it tells you which format is weak, rather than averaging it away.

## Part 3 — Your plan to 6 December

### The numbers

| Target | Amount | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary | ~800 words | Flashcards + Vocabulary by theme |
| Kanji | ~100 | 漢字読み / 表記 drills, and inside vocab |
| Grammar | Minna I, Lessons 1–25 | Lessons + Grammar drills |
| Reading | 短文 / 中文 / 情報検索 | Reading practice |
| Listening | everyday dialogue | Listening (on your phone) |

Over ~110 study days that's about **8 new words a day** and **1 kanji a day**.

> **The JLPT publishes no official vocabulary, kanji or grammar list** — deliberately,
> since 2010, to discourage list-cramming. The ~800 / ~100 figures are reconstructed from
> the pre-2010 出題基準 and observed papers. The *format* above is official; the content
> counts are a good estimate, not a syllabus.

### The one rule

**Never break the flashcards.** Even on a dead day, do your SRS reviews. Everything else
is a bonus on top.

### Your daily hour

| Min | Block | Section |
|----:|---|---|
| 15 | SRS reviews first, then ~10 new cards | Flashcards |
| 15 | Study one grammar point, then drill it | Lessons → *Drill lesson N* |
| 10 | 文字・語彙 — rotate the four types | 文字・語彙 drills |
| 15 | Reading **or** Listening, alternating | Reading practice / Listening |
| 5 | Warm-down | Sentence of the day |

Log each block on the **Today** check-in so the streak and heatmap fill in.

**Do the listening blocks on your phone.** That is the section with a hard sectional
minimum and the one you cannot practise on the laptop as it stands.

### Weekly rhythm

- **Mon–Fri** — the hour above.
- **Saturday** — lighter: consolidate the week's vocab, one reading passage.
- **Sunday** — a **mock exam**, then spend the rest of the hour on every question you got
  wrong. Read the per-大問 breakdown and pick next week's weak spot from it.
- **Minimum day** — flashcards only. Keep the streak.

### The four phases

**Phase 1 · Foundation — 15 Aug to 5 Sep (weeks 1–3)**
- Kana *cold*: read the Kana chart with rōmaji off, no hesitation.
- Build the SRS habit, 10–15 new cards a day.
- Lessons **1–5**, drilling each one straight after reading it.
- Themed vocab: Numbers, Days, Dates, Time, Greetings, Family.
- Start 漢字読み now — don't save kanji for later.
- *Milestone:* kana automatic · ~150–200 words · Lessons 1–5 ticked.

**Phase 2 · Core grammar — 6 Sep to 17 Oct (weeks 4–9)**
- Lessons **6–15** — particles, adjectives, likes, existence, counters, comparing,
  wanting, and the て-form. Lesson 14 is the hinge; everything later builds on it.
- Grammar drill every day. Add **文の組み立て** once you've met the て-form.
- Themed vocab: Colours, Body, Food, Places, Weather, Position, Adjectives, Verbs,
  Question words.
- Reading: 短文. Listening: 即時応答 and 発話表現 (the short formats) on your phone.
- *Milestone:* ~500 words · ~60 kanji · て-form automatic.

**Phase 3 · Finish the grammar — 18 Oct to 14 Nov (weeks 10–13)**
- Lessons **16–25** — て-form uses, ない-form, dictionary form, plain style, 〜と思います,
  noun-modifying clauses, とき / と, giving and receiving, たら / ても.
- Reading and listening **daily** now, not alternating. Move up to 中文 and 情報検索.
- Add **文章の文法** — it needs enough grammar to judge a whole passage.
- First full mock exams. Expect a low score; that is information, not failure.
- *Milestone:* ~750 words · ~100 kanji · all 25 lessons ticked · every 大問 attempted.

**Phase 4 · Polish — 15 Nov to 6 December (weeks 14–16)**
- **Mock 2–3× a week**, always reviewing the misses.
- Little new material. Re-run every drill, re-read every passage, sweep all 16 vocab
  themes with rōmaji off.
- Listening every single day. It has a sectional minimum and it is the least forgiving.
- Sit at least one **official past paper** end-to-end for real timing — search
  "JLPT N5 official practice workbook".
- *Milestone:* mocks consistently 60%+, with **no 大問 below 50%**.

### Knowing you're on track

- **Heatmap** dark, **countdown** not ignored.
- Grammar / 文字・語彙 / Reading bests climbing toward ~80%.
- **Mock 60%+ by mid-November.** The real pass is 80/180 (~44%), so 60% is your cushion.
- **No single 大問 stuck below 50%.** The sectional minimums mean one collapsed paper
  sinks you even with a good total — check the per-大問 table, not just the overall.

## Honest caveats

1. **This is a study companion, not a course.** It drills the formats and explains the
   grammar, but a teacher or a textbook will catch things it can't.
2. **Two listening formats are approximations.** 発話表現 shows the situation as an
   English line where the real exam shows an illustration, and 即時応答 shows its three
   replies as text where the exam speaks them. Both were the only workable substitutes
   in a single offline file.
3. **The listening banks are the thinnest** at 43 items — they'll repeat sooner than the
   200-item grammar bank. Supplement with the channels above.
4. **Progress is per-browser.** Your phone and laptop keep separate streaks; there's no
   sync.
5. **Confirm your own test date and registration deadline.** Sittings and signup windows
   vary by country, and registration usually closes months ahead.
