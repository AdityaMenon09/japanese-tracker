## 1 · Hover glosses on the lessons

Every Japanese term inside an opened lesson is now orange with a dotted underline. Hover it
(or Tab to it, or tap on mobile) and a card shows the term, its **rōmaji**, and **what it
does** in English:

> **は** · *wa* · Topic marker — "as for …". Written ha, but always pronounced wa.

**2,187 glossed terms across the 25 lessons, with zero Japanese left unglossed** — kanji
example lines included.

### How the glossary was built

Hand-typing ~2,500 entries would have been slow and inconsistent, so it is layered, and
later layers win:

1. **Grammar** (147, hand-written) — particles, the copula, and patterns. These need a
   *functional* gloss, not a translation: `の` reads "Links two nouns, owner or describer
   first — わたしの かさ = my umbrella." Machine translation cannot produce that.
2. **Verbs / adjectives** (generated) — conjugation is rule-based, so forms come from a stem
   table by the standard group I/II/III rules. Kanji spellings (食べる, 行く) are conjugated
   in lock-step with a kana twin and borrow that form's rōmaji, since the rōmaji helper
   cannot read kanji.
3. **Kanji vocabulary** (99, hand-written) — not rule-derivable.
4. **The app's own `WORDS` / `THEMES` banks** (429, harvested) — nouns and everyday words,
   so the glosses agree with what the rest of the app already teaches.

Generated set: 2,492 entries. Pruned to the **543 the lessons actually use** — 35 KB instead
of ~175 KB — then re-verified at zero gaps with the pruned set.

### Two real bugs, both found by checking rather than assuming

- **The word bank would have glossed `は` as "tooth"** and `に` as "2" — both are legitimate
  vocabulary entries that collide with the two most common particles in the language.
  Grammar now outranks vocabulary in the merge.
- **Greedy longest-match is the wrong algorithm here.** In 「吸ってはいけません」it grabs
  `はい` ("yes") and strands the `け`. Replaced with a **dynamic-programming segmentation
  that minimises unglossed characters** (then token count), which picks `は` + `いけません`.
  The browser and the verification script run the same algorithm.

### Smaller things worth remembering

- `JP_CH` had to **exclude `・` (U+30FB)** — it sits inside the katakana block, so the list
  separator in 「う・つ・る」was being highlighted as a word. The range now stops at ヺ and
  resumes at ー, keeping the long-vowel mark for ラーメン.
- The tooltip is a single element on **`<body>`**, not a child of the term: `.ls-tw` is
  `overflow-x:auto`, so a positioned child gets clipped inside the grammar tables. Verified
  it renders on top and unclipped there.
- **Weight is inherited, not forced bold.** A fully-glossed example sentence would otherwise
  become a wall of orange bold instead of reading as a sentence.
- The **"write your own sentence" block is excluded**. Its Japanese is the user's own text,
  which the glossary will never fully cover, and partial highlighting reads as broken.
- Skips `<code>` (keyboard spellings) and form elements — rewriting a `<textarea>`'s text
  node would corrupt what was typed.

## 2 · Drill banks

**The repeats were not a broken shuffle.** `sample()` is a correct Fisher–Yates. The cause
was volume: `GRAMMAR.filter(g => g.L === lesson)` returned exactly **8 questions per lesson**
and the drill draws 8, so a single-lesson run always showed every one of them.

| Bank | Was | Now |
|------|-----|-----|
| 文法形式の判断 | 200 (**8/lesson**) | **400 (16/lesson)** |
| 漢字読み | 40 | 70 |
| 表記 | 30 | 50 |
| 文脈規定 | 35 | 60 |
| 言い換え類義 | 25 | 40 |

### The validator earned its place

A script checks every item for: a blank marker present, exactly three distractors, **the
answer not also listed as one of its own distractors**, no duplicate or empty distractors,
and no duplicate questions.

It caught **40 of the new grammar questions duplicating existing ones** — written before the
original bank for lessons 11–25 had been read properly — plus 2 cross-lesson repeats. All
rewritten.

Duplicate detection compares the Japanese with the English hint stripped, *plus the answer*.
Stripping the hint alone catches "(don't take)" vs "(please don't take)"; keeping the answer
avoids false-flagging 「いま ◻ です。」(4 / 7 / 9 o'clock), which share a stem but test
よじ / しちじ / くじ.
